## DeepSeek-R1 (arXiv 2501.12948)

v1 was read as a PDF (Jan 2025). v2 (4 Jan 2026, the Nature version) was read as HTML.

**R1-Zero rewards** (v1 §2.2.2) — verified
- Rule-based rewards only: an accuracy reward and a format reward that requires `<think>` tags.
- The paper says it uses no outcome or process neural reward model, because "the neural reward model may suffer from reward hacking in the large-scale reinforcement learning process."

**R1-Zero AIME 2024** — verified; the two versions differ
- v1 (Table 2): pass@1 rises from 15.6% to 71.0%, and cons@64 reaches 86.7%.
- v2: pass@1 rises from 15.6% to **77.9%**, and 86.7% "with self-consistency decoding"; the figure uses Cons@16.
- The lesson quotes v1 and notes that the published version reports 77.9%.

**R1-Zero training curves** (v1 Fig. 2, Fig. 3) — verified
- Fig. 2: pass@1 climbs from about 0.16 to about 0.71 over roughly 8,400 steps. Each point averages 16 samples.
- Fig. 3: average response length grows from about 500 to about 9,500 tokens.
- The lesson's redraw uses approximate readings off these figures and says so.

**"Aha moment"** (v1 Table 3) — verified
- An intermediate checkpoint writes "Wait, wait. Wait. That's an aha moment I can flag here."
- v2 describes "a sudden increase in the use of the word 'wait'."

**R1-Zero weaknesses** (v1 §2.2.4) — verified: poor readability and language mixing.

**Cold start** (v1 §2.3.1) — verified
- The paper says "thousands of cold-start data," used "to prevent the early unstable cold start phase of RL training from the base model."
- The readable output format puts a summary after the reasoning.

**Language consistency reward** (v1 §2.3.2) — verified
- It is "the proportion of target language words in the CoT."
- It is summed with the accuracy reward.
- Ablations show "a slight degradation in the model's performance."

**Rejection sampling and SFT** (v1 §2.3.3) — verified
- About 600k reasoning samples, drawn from the RL checkpoint, keeping only correct ones.
- Some data is judged by DeepSeek-V3 as a generative reward model.
- Filters drop mixed languages, long paragraphs and code blocks.
- About 200k non-reasoning samples: writing, factual QA, self-cognition and translation.
- "We fine-tune DeepSeek-V3-Base for two epochs using the above curated dataset of about 800k samples."
- v2 lists 804,745 samples in total.

**Final RL stage** (v1 §2.3.4) — verified
- Rule-based rewards for reasoning data; reward models for general data.
- Helpfulness is judged on the final summary only; harmlessness on the entire response.

**GRPO settings for R1-Zero** (v2) — verified: learning rate 3e-6, KL coefficient 0.001, 16 samples per question.

**Distilled models** (v1 Table 5), AIME 2024 pass@1 — verified

| Model | AIME 2024 pass@1 |
|---|---|
| R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B | 28.9 |
| R1-Distill-Qwen-7B | 55.5 |
| R1-Distill-Qwen-14B | 69.7 |
| R1-Distill-Qwen-32B | 72.6 |
| R1-Distill-Llama-8B | 50.4 |
| R1-Distill-Llama-70B | 70.0 |

The distilled models had SFT only, with no RL stage.

**Distillation vs RL at 32B** (v1 Table 6, §4.1) — verified
- DeepSeek-R1-Zero-Qwen-32B, trained with large-scale RL for over 10k steps, scores 47.0. R1-Distill-Qwen-32B scores 72.6.
- The conclusion, verbatim: distilling more powerful models into smaller ones "yields excellent results, whereas smaller models relying on the large-scale RL ... require enormous computational power and may not even achieve the performance of distillation"; and "advancing beyond the boundaries of intelligence may still require more powerful base models and larger-scale reinforcement learning."

**Unsuccessful attempts: PRM** (v1 §4.2) — verified. The paper lists three limitations:
1. it is hard to define a fine-grained step;
2. it is hard to judge whether an intermediate step is correct (automatic annotation is unsatisfactory, and manual annotation does not scale);
3. a model-based PRM "inevitably leads to reward hacking", and retraining it costs resources.

The paper still says a PRM "demonstrates a good ability to rerank the top-N responses generated by the model or assist in guided search."

**Unsuccessful attempts: MCTS** — verified. It did not scale: the search space is huge, and the value model is hard to train.

**Final R1, AIME 2024 pass@1** (Table 4) — verified: 79.8.

## Process rewards

**Lightman et al. 2023, "Let's Verify Step by Step"** (2305.20050) — verified
- Process supervision beats outcome supervision when reward models are used for **best-of-N search**.
- At best-of-1860 on a 500-problem subset of MATH: PRM 78.2%, ORM 72.4%, majority voting 69.6%.
- PRM800K contains 800k step-level human labels.

**Math-Shepherd** (2312.08935) — verified (abstract)
- Process supervision data is constructed automatically, without manual annotation.
- It is used both to rerank outputs and for step-by-step PPO.
- With Mistral-7B, step-by-step PPO moves GSM8K from 77.9 to 84.1 and MATH from 28.6 to 33.0.
- *The exact labelling method (continuing from a step and checking the answer) was not checked in the full paper, so the lesson says only "automatically, from rollouts" (softened).*

## Test-time scaling

**s1** (2501.19393) — verified
- s1K has 1,000 questions with reasoning traces; SFT is applied to Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct.
- "Budget forcing" ends thinking early, or lengthens it by "appending 'Wait' multiple times" when the model tries to stop.
- The result exceeds o1-preview on competition math by up to 27%.

## GRPO fixes

**DAPO** (2503.14476) — verified
- With ε = 0.2, tokens at probability 0.01 and 0.9 can rise at most to "0.012 and 1.08". DAPO uses ε_low = 0.2 and ε_high = 0.28.
- Dynamic sampling: "over-sample and filter out prompts with the accuracy equal to 1 and 0."
- The KL term is removed, because "the model distribution can diverge significantly from the initial model."
- AIME 50 with Qwen2.5-32B, against 47 for DeepSeek-R1-Zero-Qwen-32B, in 50% of the training steps.

**Dr. GRPO** (2503.20783) — verified (abstract)
- "DeepSeek-V3-Base already exhibit 'Aha moment'."
- Qwen2.5 base models reason well even without templates.
- GRPO has an optimization bias that "artificially increases response length (especially for incorrect outputs)."
- 43.3% on AIME 2024 with a 7B base model.
- *Correction to the plan:* the "aha already in the base model" finding is about **DeepSeek-V3-Base**, not Qwen.

## Does RL teach or elicit?

**Yue et al.** (2504.13837, NeurIPS 2025 oral) — verified (abstract)
- RLVR models win at small k (e.g. k = 1); "the base models achieve a higher pass@k score when k is large."
- Reasoning "originate[s] from and [is] bounded by the base model."
- Distillation "can introduce new reasoning patterns from the teacher."

**Qwen3 Technical Report** (2505.09388, §4.5 and Table 21) — verified
- Strong-to-weak distillation runs in two phases: off-policy (teacher outputs in /think and /no_think modes), then on-policy (the student samples, and its logits are aligned with the teacher's by minimizing KL). The teachers are Qwen3-32B or Qwen3-235B-A22B.
- Table 21, Qwen3-8B, starting from the same off-policy-distilled checkpoint:

  | Method | AIME'24 | AIME'25 | GPU hours | AIME'24 pass@64 |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | + RL | 67.6 | 55.5 | 17,920 | 90.0 (unchanged from the checkpoint) |
  | + on-policy distillation | 74.4 | 65.5 | 1,800 | 93.3 |

- The report states that RL "does not lead to any improvement in pass@64," while distillation does.
- The off-policy checkpoint itself scores 55.0 on AIME'24, as quoted in the Thinking Machines post.
- *Correction:* research-technical.md §7 credits 74.4 vs 67.6 to Thinking Machines' own runs. The numbers come from the Qwen3 report, and the Thinking Machines post quotes them.

## On-policy distillation

**Thinking Machines, "On-Policy Distillation" (2025)** — verified
- "We set the per-token advantage to the negative reverse KL"; "we use a discount factor of zero."
- The teacher needs "just a single forward pass" to compute log-probabilities.
- In the authors' own runs, a 400k-SFT checkpoint at 60% on AIME'24 reaches 70% in about 150 steps of on-policy distillation.
- After personalization mid-training on a 70/30 mix, IF-eval rose from 79% to 83% with on-policy distillation; the original model scored 85%.
- Off-policy distillation is described as dense but off-policy, RL as on-policy but sparse, and on-policy distillation as on-policy and dense.
- The post does **not** say a shared tokenizer is required. The lesson says only that the teacher must score the student's tokens, which in practice means the same tokenizer, or an alignment method such as GOLD/ULD.

**Multi-teacher on-policy distillation (MOPD)** (Interconnects, 16 Jun 2026, a podcast summary) — verified as reported
- Train several domain-specialist teachers, then a student on its own samples, minimizing reverse KL to the relevant teacher.
- MiMo Flash v2 introduced the term; "DeepSeek V4 & Nemotron 3 Ultra scale it to >10 teachers."
- NVIDIA found that "teacher models trained with substantially different training pipelines cannot be effectively combined."
- GLM-5 uses a simpler SFT-then-RL pipeline without MOPD.
- *Caveat (research-adoption):* this comes from an interview, not from the labs' own reports.

## Graders and managed RFT

**Microsoft Foundry RFT** (MS Learn, updated 2026-10-08) — verified
- Grader types: string check (`eq`, `ne`, `like`, `ilike`), text similarity (fuzzy_match, BLEU, ROUGE…), model grader (`score_model` with a rubric), Python grader (`grade(sample, item)`, run without network access), and Multi-Grader.
- The docs advise: "Check that superficial responses can't earn a high score without solving the task," and "High grader rewards alone don't establish that the model solves your task."

**Amazon Bedrock RFT** (AWS docs) — verified
- Supported models: Nova 2 Lite, gpt-oss-20B and Qwen3 32B.
- Rewards come from custom code in Lambda, or from a model-as-a-judge grader. Training uses GRPO.
- Best practices:
  - start with 100–200 examples;
  - if rewards are consistently 0%, do SFT first; if they are above 95%, RFT may be unnecessary;
  - watch for training reward rising while validation reward falls.

**Fireworks RFT** (blog) — verified
- The user writes Python evaluator code ("reward-kit").
- Supports Llama, Phi, Qwen 2.5/3, DeepSeek V3/R1. Builds on RLVR.
- The algorithm is not named in the post.

**Reward hacking in code** (Lilian Weng, "Reward Hacking", 2024) — verified
- Denison et al. 2024 (2406.10162): a model learned to change unit tests and, rarely, to overwrite its reward.
- Wen et al. 2024 (2409.12822): RLHF-trained models hacked human-written unit tests.

**LLM-as-judge biases** (Zheng et al. 2023, 2306.05685) — verified: position, verbosity and self-enhancement biases, and limited reasoning. GPT-4 judges agree with humans over 80% of the time.

## TRL v1.15 (checked against docs and source, 2026-10-10)

**GRPOConfig** — verified
- Defaults: `loss_type='dapo'`, `beta=0.0` (the reference model is not loaded), `epsilon=0.2`, `epsilon_high=None`, `scale_rewards='group'`, `mask_truncated_completions=False`, `importance_sampling_level='token'`, `num_generations=8`, `learning_rate=1e-6`.
- vLLM importance-sampling correction is on by default (`clip_max=3.0`).
- Loss types: grpo, dr_grpo, dapo, bnpo, cispo, sapo, luspo, vespo.
- Logged metrics include `frac_reward_zero_std`, `completions/clipped_ratio`, `entropy`, `clip_ratio/*` and `sampling/*`.
- Reward functions receive the dataset columns as keyword arguments, plus an `environments` keyword argument when an environment is configured. The effective batch size must be divisible by `num_generations`.

**DPOConfig** — verified
- `beta=0.1`, `learning_rate=1e-6`, `max_length=1024` (only `keep_start` truncation).
- `loss_type` is a list, combined using `loss_weights`, e.g. `["sigmoid","sft"]`.
- No `rpo_alpha` and no `max_prompt_length`. bf16 is on by default when fp16 is not set.
- Logged metrics: `logps/chosen`, `logps/rejected`, `rewards/*`.

**GKD and GOLD** — verified
- Both live in `trl.experimental`.
- GKD: `lmbda` is the fraction of on-policy data; `beta` = 0 gives forward KL and `beta` = 1 reverse KL; generation temperature defaults to 0.9.
- GOLD distills across tokenizers (`use_uld_loss`, `teacher_tokenizer_name_or_path`).
